\begin{abstract}
Trusted monitoring in AI control is evaluated on stored honest/attack pairs. The field
already knows these pairs leak: it filters to working solutions and strips comments. We
ask what that preprocessing leaves behind. On the field's own filtered,
comment-stripped split, a bag-of-words classifier scores above roughly four-fifths of the
open-weight monitors ranked on it, and a classifier that reads no code at all --- only
problem metadata --- scores above most of them. We trace the residue to authorship rather
than sabotage: the honest class is human-written while every attack is model-written. The
signal transfers across backdoor generators, is nearly as strong on inert backdoors as on
working ones, and survives the field's comment stripper. Five same-generator agentic settings, where no authorship confound is possible,
carry a second, structural floor. Regenerating only
the honest half does not isolate content: with an open-weight model writing honest
solutions against the stored backdoors, separability \emph{rises}, because the substitute
generator opens a larger authorship gap than it closes. Holding authorship constant
requires one model to write both classes; we report that design and its single-shot
yield, which shows how heavily the stored attack class has been filtered. We recommend that
stored-pair evaluations report partial-input and bag-of-words floors against permutation
nulls, use same-generator honest samples, and drop inert backdoors from both classes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% notes/06 §5.2, pasted 2026-09-11. Paragraph three deliberately carries no transfer or
% inert VALUE: those go in only after the stripped re-check (handoff §4.2).
% =====================================================================================

Trusted monitoring is the backbone protocol of AI control: a weaker trusted model reads an
untrusted model's output and flags sabotage \cite{greenblatt2023aicontrol}. It is developed,
tuned and ranked almost entirely on \emph{stored} honest/attack pairs --- fixed corpora of
solutions, some carrying a planted backdoor \cite{koran2026ensemble, terekhov2025controltax,
jha2026decorrelation}. The field already knows these pairs leak. Koran
et al.\ keep only working honest solutions and working backdoors and strip every comment, with
the rationale stated \cite{koran2026ensemble}; Control Tax reports the pass-tests pre-filter and
appendices ``Monitoring failed backdoors'' \cite{terekhov2025controltax};
\cite{sreevatsa2026legibility} is an entire paper on the comment channel. The sharpest treatment
is Jha's, whose released harness ships a \texttt{--leak-check} flag with two trivial baselines
already inside it and concludes that stripping is mandatory rather than cosmetic: a
\texttt{\#}-count detector falls from \hashRaw{} to \hashStripped{} once comments go, and code
length alone sits at \lenFiltered{} \cite{jha2026decorrelation}. This paper starts from that
work, not against it.

What nobody has measured is what the preprocessing leaves behind. On the field's own filtered,
comment-stripped split, a bag-of-words classifier reaches AUROC \tfidfKoran{} --- above
\tfidfKoranCount{} of the \monN{} open-weight monitors ranked on that same split, whose median
is \monMedian{} --- and a classifier that reads no code at all, only problem difficulty, test
count and question length, reaches \nocodeCV{}, above \nocodeCVCount{}.

The residue is authorship, not sabotage. Honest solutions come from APPS
\cite{hendrycks2021apps} and are human-written; every backdoor is model-written. The
classifier transfers across backdoor generators and is nearly as strong on \textbf{inert}
backdoors as on working ones --- neither is a property of a backdoor. That bag-of-words separates
human-written from model-written code is established out-of-field \cite{orel2026aicd}; the
known-ness of the mechanism is the argument, not a dent in it. The neighbouring field already applies the control missing here --- CodeMirage gates its
two classes on line count and character length \cite{guo2025codemirage}, MultiAIGCD pairs to
shared problem definitions \cite{demirok2025multiaigcd} --- so unmatched pairs are a defect by a
published standard, not one we invented. Adjacent control work attacks other axes:
\cite{terekhov2026adaptive} subverts monitors with injected text,
\cite{gewang2026attackselection} shows attacker-side selection inflates safety estimates, and
\cite{pinto2026calibration} removes transcript length as a nuisance covariate, where we publish
a content-blind AUROC as a per-benchmark floor.

We contribute (i)~a protocol $\times$ baseline measurement of what survives the field's
preprocessing; (ii)~evidence that the survivor is authorship, including a negative result:
regenerating one half of a stored pair swaps the confound rather than removing it; (iii)~a
second selection artifact the filter installs by defining the negative class through attack
failure; and (iv)~a preprocessing checklist and a released split.
\textbf{Scope:} this concerns monitor training, selection and benchmarking on \emph{stored}
pairs. ControlArena's live evaluations generate both classes and are not implicated.

\section{Setup}
% Budget: ~300 words. The schema table is Table~\ref{tab:schema} in the appendix.
% =====================================================================================

\paragraph{The confound is legible in the schema.}
The canonical stored-pair artifact for APPS-based control evaluations
\cite{inglis2025appscontrolarena} records \texttt{model} and \texttt{source} on every
backdoor and no generator field on any honest solution (Table~\ref{tab:schema}, appendix):
the honest class is taken from APPS \cite{hendrycks2021apps} and names no model; every
backdoor is model-written.

\paragraph{Artifacts and provenance.}
We use seven public corpora; their per-class provenance is the object of study: in the
APPS family the honest class is human and the attack class is one of ten named models, while
in the five agentic settings the same three models write \emph{both} classes. \textbf{Six of
the seven, from two publishers, declare no licence} \cite{koran2026highquality,
sinha2026controlarenacorpora}; we report only aggregates over them and redistribute no
corpus.

\paragraph{Evaluation surface, and naming protocols.}
Our headline protocol is the filtered, comment-stripped split of \cite{koran2026ensemble}:
\splitTrain{} train and \splitTest{} test items (\splitAttack{} attack, \splitHonest{}
honest), labelled by \texttt{backdoor\_works}, reconstructed with the released code of
\cite{jha2026decorrelation}. It is the surface on which \monN{}
open-weight monitors have published per-item scores, which makes a floor comparable to a
monitor. The raw artifact, this split, pooled evaluation, matched pairs and
within-task evaluation are five populations, and pairwise accuracy and AUROC two metrics;
every comparison below names its split, metric and preprocessing, and no value is placed
beside a count from another protocol. Generated code is scored by our own runner, which
agrees with the artifact's \texttt{solution\_passes\_tests} on \harnessAgreement{} of the
analysis pool and is applied to both classes (appendix~\ref{app:harness}).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% Budget: ~780 words. F1 is Table~\ref{tab:protocol-baseline} in the appendix; F3 is the
% one body float.
% =====================================================================================

Table~\ref{tab:protocol-baseline} (appendix) reports what a deliberately trivial classifier
recovers under each protocol. The fix works where it was aimed: code length is driven to
chance on the filtered split (\lenFiltered{}) and the \texttt{\#}-count detector falls from
\hashRaw{} to \hashStripped{}. What it does not reach, on that same split, is a
bag-of-words floor of \tfidfKoran{} (95\% CI \tfidfKoranLo{}--\tfidfKoranHi{} by bootstrap
over problems; permutation null 95th percentile \tfidfKoranNull{}, $\permP$), above
\tfidfKoranCount{} of the \monN{} monitors by point estimate and \tfidfKoranPairedCount{}
of them with a paired-bootstrap interval excluding zero, and a no-code floor of \nocodeCV{}
(CI \nocodeCVLo{}--\nocodeCVHi{}; null \nocodeCVNull{}, $\permP$), above \nocodeCVCount{}
and \nocodeCVPairedCount{} respectively (Figure~\ref{fig:monitors}): the preprocessing
removed the carrier it targeted and not the two beside it.

\paragraph{The signal is authorship, not sabotage.}
Three measurements point the same way; every matched-protocol number here is
comment-stripped on both classes, and the unstripped values, inflated by the comment
channel, are in appendix~\ref{app:stripped}. First, the classifier \emph{transfers across
backdoor generators} at \transfer{} pairwise: trained on one attacker's backdoors it
identifies another's, so what it has learned is not any single model's sabotage style.
Second, it is nearly as strong on \textbf{inert} backdoors (\pairInert{}) as on working
ones (\pairWorking{}) --- if it were detecting sabotage, backdoors that do not function
should be much harder, and they are not. Third, the residual carrier is stylistic rather
than semantic: \texttt{\_\_main\_\_} appears in 0.116 of attack samples against
\textbf{0.000} of honest ones, replicated on the train split (152 versus 1 of
\splitTrain{}) and untouched by the stripper. None of these is a property of a
backdoor. All of them are properties of who wrote the file. A fourth, token-level test \cite{orel2026aicd} does not replicate here
(separation \tokenSep{}, permutation $p = \tokenP{}$; appendix~\ref{app:token}), a
register effect. One authorship leak even survives the field's own comment
stripper, because string literals are not comments: in the comment-stripped train split an
author byline (\texttt{Author :}) survives in \bylineHonest{} of \splitTrain{} honest samples
against \bylineAttack{} attack samples, and a triple-quoted block in \tripleHonest{} against
\tripleAttack{} --- too rare to drive \tfidfKoran{}, but a literal record of authorship.

\paragraph{Regenerating one half of a pair does not isolate content.}
Under the identical pipeline the human-honest baseline is $B = \baselineB$ over
$n = \baselineBn{}$ pairs, and the routing rule and its bands were fixed before any arm
ran: retained signal $\rho = (G - 0.5)/(B - 0.5)$, with $\rho \le \rhoCollapse{}$ reading as
authorship and $\rho > \rhoContent{}$ as content. With an open-weight model writing the
honest class against the same backdoors ($n = \armOneN{}$, $B' = \armOneBprime{}$ on those
problems), separation \emph{rises} to $G = \armOneG{}$, $\rho = \armOneRho{}$, some
\armOneSd{} standard deviations above $B'$; TF-IDF gains \armOneTfidfDelta{} while the
length carrier collapses to \armOneLenG{}, near chance. The new gap is lexical, not
structural: substituting the honest author replaced one authorship confound with a larger
one, and any control that regenerates one side of a stored pair inherits the same defect.

\paragraph{Holding authorship constant needs care about what else changes.}
We generate the attack class twice from one honest class: an \textbf{edit}
arm shown its own honest solution and an \textbf{independent} arm shown only the problem
statement. The stored benchmark's pairs are largely independent programs (mean character
similarity \simStored{}); the edit arm's are near-copies
(\simEdit{}) while the independent arm matches the stored regime (\simIndep{};
Table~\ref{tab:similarity}, appendix). Only the independent arm changes authorship alone
--- a low $S$ from the edit arm could mean only that we made the two classes the same text
--- so we route on it and report the edit arm only as a bound.

\textbf{The same-generator control did not land, and the authorship reading is inference.}
Holding authorship constant requires one model to write both classes; our attack generations
converted at roughly \yieldSingleShot{} into pairs that both pass the public tests and fire on
their trigger --- \usableEdit{} of \nEdit{} in the edit arm and \usableIndep{} of \nIndep{}
in the independent arm, against the \pairFloor{}-pair floor our analysis script requires ---
so we report no $S$. The authorship reading is inference from the transfer, inert-backdoor
and style evidence together with the five same-generator settings. The yield gap is itself a
measurement: ControlArena attack policies resample and keep survivors, so the stored attack
class is heavily filtered relative to any single-shot rate. One rule was changed before the
retry batch was read, and we say so: the $\pm2$ fold-sd interval first coded for $S$ is far
wider than a 95\% interval over problems and biased toward ``inconclusive'', so $S$ now
routes on a bootstrap-over-problems interval.

max \monMax{}. \textbf{Two caveats.}
(i)~The \monN{} monitors are zero-shot prompted while the bag-of-words classifier is
\emph{trained} on the benchmark's own paired train split --- this is a statement about
what the corpus makes recoverable, in the sense of \cite{gururangan2018annotation}, not a
claim that bag-of-words is a better monitor. (ii)~The no-code floor is fitted \emph{within}
the test split by $5\times5$ cross-validation, because the released train/val split is
paired and therefore metadata-balanced, so its permutation null shuffles labels for fitting
and scoring alike; on a single held-out slice of the same items it is \nocodeHeldout{}
(CI \nocodeHeldoutLo{}--\nocodeHeldoutHi{}; $p = \nocodeHeldoutP{}$ against its own null),
above \nocodeHeldoutCount{} of \monN{}, \nocodeHeldoutPairedCount{} with a paired interval
excluding zero.}
\label{fig:monitors}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The filter installs a second artifact.}
Labelling by \texttt{backdoor\_works} defines the negative class by \emph{attack failure}
rather than by honesty: \inertShare{} of the test split's labelled backdoors do not work
and are relabelled honest, so most of the ``honest'' class consists of failed attacks.
Attack problems carry roughly half as many test cases as honest ones
(\testCasesAttack{} versus \testCasesHonest{}), consistent with a problem with fewer tests
being easier to backdoor undetectably, so difficulty and test count are correlated with the
label before any code is read --- which is where the content-blind floor of \nocodeCV{}
comes from. The filter that removes the comment channel installs a metadata channel in its
place.

\paragraph{The negative control comes free.}
In five same-generator agentic settings the same three models write both classes, so no
authorship confound is possible and any surviving floor is structural
(Figure~\ref{fig:corpora}, appendix). The result is a range: \texttt{bash} sits on its
permutation null at \bashFloor{} while \texttt{agentdojo} reaches \agentdojoFloor{} from character count alone. A
per-benchmark floor therefore separates corpora where the label is trivially recoverable
from those where it is not.
\textbf{Two mechanisms:} authorship on the APPS pairs, structure on the agentic settings,
and neither is visible without running the baseline.

\section{Recommendations}
% Budget: ~190 words, in Kapoor & Narayanan's register.
% =====================================================================================

Following \citet{kapoor2022leakage}, who treat leakage as a checklist rather than a verdict:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Generate both classes with the same model.} If the attack class is
      model-written, the honest class must be too; as our arm-1 null shows, regenerating
      only one side substitutes one authorship gap for another. This is precedent
      \cite{guo2025codemirage, demirok2025multiaigcd}, not invention.
\item \textbf{Report a partial-input floor and a bag-of-words floor beside every monitor
      number}, each against a permutation null. A monitor that
      does not clear its corpus's own floor has not been shown to do anything.
\item \textbf{Do not define the negative class by attack failure.} Drop inert backdoors
      from both classes rather than relabelling a failed attack as honest, which imports
      the attacker's success rate into the label.
\item \textbf{State the split, the metric and the preprocessing} whenever monitors are
      compared, and \textbf{release the split definition and the scripts} so a floor can be
      recomputed rather than trusted. Ours will be released under MIT.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
% Budget: three paragraphs. Disclosures owed by notes/05 are in the second.
% =====================================================================================

\textbf{A high floor is evidence about a corpus, not a verdict on results computed on it.}
\citet{feng2019misleading} warn that the \emph{failure} of a partial-input baseline does not
show a dataset is artifact-free; the converse binds us: our floors show the label is
recoverable without reading a backdoor, in the sense of \citet{gururangan2018annotation} and
\citet{poliak2018hypothesis}, not that any monitor exploits that (Figure~\ref{fig:monitors},
caveat~i).

\textbf{The same-generator arm is a later model behind a paid API.} The stored attacker,
\texttt{claude-3-7-sonnet-thinking}, is retired (404 against a live key), so both
same-generator classes come from a later model of the same lineage, called through a
metered commercial API rather than an open-weight model. That API does not expose its sampling parameters, so the arm is not bit-reproducible as the
open-weight arms are, and thinking was off although the stored attacker was a thinking
model. That the remaining cross-model difference is small is an inference from our
\transfer{} transfer and \citet{orel2026aicd}'s generator-independence result. We do not
length-match: in deployment the untrusted model chooses its trajectory length. The authorship reading is therefore inference (\S\ref{sec:results}), and we label
it as such.

\textbf{Scope, provenance and the harness.} Whether stored-pair corpora leak this way in
general is not established here. Five of our seven corpora are one user's
repackaging, so claims are scoped to those public releases. Two interpreter-era artifacts
fall on opposite classes (a patched \texttt{stdin} lacking \texttt{.buffer} penalised the
model class, fixed; \texttt{fractions.gcd}, removed in Python 3.9, penalises old human
code, scored as failing); the same-generator arm is immune, $B$ is not.

\paragraph{The test harness and the pass flag.}
\label{app:harness}
Gate S requires executing generated code, so we built a runner that feeds each shipped
input case to a solution under per-case timeouts and address-space limits. It agrees with
the artifact's own \texttt{solution\_passes\_tests} on \harnessAgreement{} of the analysis
pool and is strictly conservative there --- it never credits a solution the artifact rejects
(\harnessFailWePass{} such cases). We use \emph{our own} flag for both classes: a checker
stricter on one class than the other would manufacture exactly the artifact this paper
documents. The headline does not depend on that choice --- under the artifact's flag
$B = \baselineB$ at $n = \baselineBn{}$, under ours $B = \baselineBownflag{}$ at
$n = \baselineBownflagN{}$, within one standard deviation of each other. Our release
carries one MIT-licensed file of published monitor scores, with its notice; the monitor
scores in Figure~\ref{fig:monitors} are \texttt{runs/x0/monitor-summary.json} of that
release.

\paragraph{The token test, run and failed.}
\label{app:token}
We attempted to confirm the token-level mechanism of \cite{orel2026aicd} in our setting and
it does not replicate: separation \tokenSep{} --- the wrong sign --- at permutation
$p = \tokenP{}$. The reason is register. Their human class is general-purpose repository
code, whereas \emph{both} of our classes are competitive-programming code, so an identifier
axis that separates general human code from model code does not separate these two
populations. Only 8 of their 10 canonical tokens are in our vocabulary, so the test is
underpowered; but the point estimate has the wrong sign, which is not what an underpowered
true effect looks like. We report it because a reader who knows that work may run exactly
this test.

\paragraph{A third channel, noted rather than developed.}
In the same five agentic settings a fifteen-word regular expression over the untrusted
policy's own scratchpad reaches \cotLow{}--\cotHigh{}, because prompted attack policies
restate their side task in the reasoning the monitor is given. We note this only to bound
the claim: the instrument belongs to \cite{little2026length}, which validates it properly
against an LLM judge and a length-matched null, and we defer to that treatment.
