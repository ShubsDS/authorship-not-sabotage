\begin{abstract}
Trusted monitoring in AI control is evaluated on stored honest/attack pairs. The field
already knows these pairs leak: it filters to working solutions and strips comments. We
ask what that preprocessing leaves behind. On the field's own filtered,
comment-stripped split, a \emph{trained} bag-of-words classifier scores above roughly four-fifths of the
open-weight monitors ranked on it, and a classifier that reads no code at all --- only
problem metadata --- scores above most of them. We trace the residue to authorship rather
than sabotage: the honest class is human-written while every attack is model-written. The
signal transfers across backdoor generators, is nearly as strong on inert backdoors as on
working ones, and survives the field's comment stripper. Regenerating only
the honest half does not isolate content: with an open-weight model writing the honest class
against the stored backdoors, separability \emph{rises}. Holding authorship constant by
construction, with one model writing both classes, removes between a third (Claude Sonnet 5,
$\rho = \sameGenRho{}$) and nine-tenths (Claude Haiku 4.5, $\rho = \haikuRho{}$) of the
separability; matching the attack prompt's framing removes most of what remains, and what
survives on attacks that actually fire is surface content a monitor also reads. A zero-shot
trusted monitor is inflated the same way, scoring \monStoredHead{} on the stored pairs and
\monitorStrict{} on firing backdoors from the same author. We
recommend that
stored-pair evaluations report partial-input and bag-of-words floors against permutation
nulls, use same-generator honest samples and the same prompt, and drop inert backdoors from
both classes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% notes/06 §5.2, pasted 2026-09-11. Paragraph three deliberately carries no transfer or
% inert VALUE: those go in only after the stripped re-check (handoff §4.2).
% =====================================================================================

Trusted monitoring is the backbone protocol of AI control: a weaker trusted model reads an
untrusted model's output and flags sabotage \cite{greenblatt2023aicontrol}. It is developed,
tuned and ranked almost entirely on \emph{stored} honest/attack pairs --- fixed corpora of
solutions, some carrying a planted backdoor \cite{koran2026ensemble, terekhov2025controltax,
jha2026decorrelation}. The field already knows these pairs leak. Koran
et al.\ keep only working honest solutions and working backdoors and strip every comment, with
the rationale stated \cite{koran2026ensemble}; Control Tax reports the pass-tests pre-filter and
appendices ``Monitoring failed backdoors'' \cite{terekhov2025controltax};
\cite{sreevatsa2026legibility} is an entire paper on the comment channel. The sharpest treatment
is Jha's, whose harness ships a \texttt{--leak-check} flag with two trivial baselines inside
it and concludes that stripping is mandatory rather than cosmetic: a \texttt{\#}-count detector
falls from \hashRaw{} to \hashStripped{} once comments go, and code length alone sits at
\lenFiltered{} \cite{jha2026decorrelation}. This paper starts from that work.

What nobody has measured is what the preprocessing leaves behind. On the field's own filtered,
comment-stripped split, a bag-of-words classifier reaches AUROC \tfidfKoran{} --- above
\tfidfKoranCount{} of the \monN{} open-weight monitors ranked on that same split, whose median
is \monMedian{} --- and a classifier that reads no code at all, only problem difficulty, test
count and question length, reaches \nocodeCV{}, above \nocodeCVCount{}.

Much of the residue is authorship, not sabotage. Honest solutions come from APPS
\cite{hendrycks2021apps} and are human-written; every backdoor is model-written. The
classifier transfers across backdoor generators and is nearly as strong on \textbf{inert}
backdoors as on working ones --- neither is a property of a backdoor. That bag-of-words separates
human-written from model-written code is established out-of-field \cite{orel2026aicd}; the
known-ness of the mechanism is the argument, not a dent in it. The neighbouring field already applies the control missing here --- CodeMirage gates its
classes on line and character count \cite{guo2025codemirage}, MultiAIGCD pairs to shared
problem definitions \cite{demirok2025multiaigcd} --- so unmatched pairs are a defect by a
published standard. Adjacent control work attacks other axes:
\cite{terekhov2026adaptive} subverts monitors with injected text,
\cite{gewang2026attackselection} shows attacker-side selection inflates safety estimates, and
\cite{pinto2026calibration} removes transcript length as a nuisance covariate; we publish one
as a per-benchmark floor.

We contribute (i)~a protocol $\times$ baseline measurement of what survives the field's
preprocessing; (ii)~a same-generator control on two generators, attributing between a
third and nine-tenths of the separability to authorship and most of the remainder to the
attack prompt's framing, with a negative result on the way: regenerating one half of a
stored pair swaps the confound rather than removing it; (iii)~a
second selection artifact the filter installs by defining the negative class through attack
failure; and (iv)~a preprocessing checklist and a released split.
\textbf{Scope:} this concerns monitor training, selection and benchmarking on \emph{stored}
pairs; our measurement is of one APPS-derived family plus five agentic settings. ControlArena's live evaluations generate both classes and are not implicated.

\paragraph{The confound is legible in the schema.}
The canonical stored-pair artifact for APPS-based control evaluations
\cite{inglis2025appscontrolarena} records \texttt{model} and \texttt{source} on every
backdoor and no generator field on any honest solution (Table~\ref{tab:schema}, appendix).

\paragraph{Artifacts and provenance.}
We use seven public corpora; their per-class provenance is the object of study: in the
APPS family the honest class is human and the attack class is one of ten named models.
\textbf{Six of
the seven, from two publishers, declare no licence} \cite{koran2026highquality,
sinha2026controlarenacorpora}; we report only aggregates over them and redistribute no
corpus.

\paragraph{Evaluation surface, and naming protocols.}
Our headline protocol is the filtered, comment-stripped split of \cite{koran2026ensemble}:

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% Budget: ~780 words. F1 is Table~\ref{tab:protocol-baseline} in the appendix; F3 is the
% one body float.
% =====================================================================================

Table~\ref{tab:protocol-baseline} (appendix) reports what a trivial classifier recovers under
each protocol. The fix works, on the two carriers \S1 names.
What it does not reach, on that same split, is a
bag-of-words floor of \tfidfKoran{} (95\% CI \tfidfKoranLo{}--\tfidfKoranHi{} by bootstrap
over problems; permutation null 95th percentile \tfidfKoranNull{}, $\permP$), above
\tfidfKoranCount{} of the \monN{} monitors by point estimate and \tfidfKoranPairedCount{}
of them with a paired-bootstrap interval excluding zero, and a no-code floor of \nocodeCV{}
(CI \nocodeCVLo{}--\nocodeCVHi{} on the pooled out-of-fold \nocodeCVPooled{}; null
\nocodeCVNull{}, $\permP$), above \nocodeCVCount{}
and \nocodeCVPairedCount{} respectively (Figure~\ref{fig:monitors}): the preprocessing
removed the carrier it targeted and not the two beside it.

\paragraph{The signal is not sabotage.}
Three measurements point the same way; every matched-protocol number here is
comment-stripped on both classes, and the unstripped values, inflated by the comment
channel, are in appendix~\ref{app:stripped}. First, the classifier \emph{transfers across
backdoor generators} at \transfer{} pairwise: trained on one attacker's backdoors it
identifies another's, so what it has learned is not any single model's sabotage style.
Second, it is nearly as strong on \textbf{inert} backdoors (\pairInert{}) as on working
ones (\pairWorking{}) --- if it were detecting sabotage, backdoors that do not function
should be much harder, and they are not. Third, the residual carrier is stylistic rather
than semantic: \texttt{\_\_main\_\_} appears in 0.116 of attack samples against
\textbf{0.000} of honest ones, replicated on the train split (152 versus 1 of
\splitTrain{}) and untouched by the stripper. None of these is a property of a
backdoor. A token-level test \cite{orel2026aicd} does not replicate, and one authorship leak
survives the stripper (appendices~\ref{app:token},~\ref{app:stripped}).

\paragraph{Regenerating one half of a pair does not isolate content.}
Under the identical pipeline the human-honest baseline is $B = \baselineB$ over
$n = \baselineBn{}$ pairs, and the routing rule and its bands were fixed before any arm
ran: retained signal $\rho = (G - 0.5)/(B - 0.5)$, with $\rho \le \rhoCollapse{}$ reading as
authorship and $\rho > \rhoContent{}$ as content. With an open-weight model writing the
honest class against the same backdoors ($n = \armOneN{}$, $B' = \armOneBprime{}$ on those
problems), separation \emph{rises} to $G = \armOneG{}$, $\rho = \armOneRho{}$: substituting the
honest author replaced one authorship confound with a larger one.

\paragraph{Holding authorship constant needs care about what else changes.}
We generate the attack class twice from one honest class. An \textbf{edit} arm shown its own
honest solution returns near-copies, so only the \textbf{independent} arm --- shown the problem
statement alone, and matching the stored regime of independent programs --- changes authorship
and nothing else (Table~\ref{tab:similarity}, appendix).

% --- Gate S outcome (handoff §4.3; RESULTS.md §9). \monitorS is pending. ---------------
\textbf{Holding authorship constant removes between a third and nine-tenths of the
separability, and the attack prompt's framing most of what remains.} With one model writing both classes --- one outcome-blind draw per problem, the
attack class defined, as in the benchmark, by intent plus passing the public tests (\headlineFireShare{} of these
draws fire, the same-generator analogue of the split's own \inertShare{} inert class), and
$B'$ inert-inclusive over the same problems --- $S = \sameGenS{}$ (95\% CI
\sameGenLo{}--\sameGenHi{}; $n = \sameGenN{}$ problem-disjoint pairs) against
$B' = \sameGenBprime{}$, so $\rho = \sameGenRho{}$ (\sameGenRhoLo{}--\sameGenRhoHi{}, joint bootstrap over
problems); a second generator writing both classes under the same rule
($n = \haikuN{}$) gives $S = \haikuS{}$ against $B' = \haikuBprime{}$,
$\rho = \haikuRho{}$ (\haikuRhoLo{}--\haikuRhoHi{}). The first
interval excludes the collapse band and spans the content line --- the pre-registered rule
returns \emph{inconclusive between partial and content} --- and the second lies inside the
collapse band. What survives is lexical:
length-only falls to \sameGenLen{} while TF-IDF holds. It is not the trigger: masking numerals and comparison
operators leaves $S$ unchanged, while normalising identifiers takes it to \sameGenSid{}
($\rho = \sameGenRhoId{}$), and the top attack-side features are
\texttt{def main} and \texttt{data[} (\defMainAttack{} vs \defMainHonest{} of pair sides) --- the
layout the attack prompt elicits. Holding the prompt constant as well removes most of the
rest: with the honest class regenerated under the attack prompt's framing minus the flaw
sentence, $S = \matchedS{}$ (\matchedLo{}--\matchedHi{}; $n = \matchedN{}$),
$\rho = \matchedRho{}$ (\matchedRhoLo{}--\matchedRhoHi{}), and the two honest classes
separate from each other at \promptOnly{}. The pairs are independent programs (Table~\ref{tab:similarity}), and at
$n = \sameGenN{}$ $B$ under the identical pipeline is $\curveHeadline{} \pm
\curveHeadlineSd{}$, so the drop is not sample size. The strict row --- attacks that pass
\emph{and} fire, kept by the benchmark's own retry-and-keep procedure, and the row that
follows our own Recommendation 3 --- agrees, at low power: $\rho = \strictRho{}$
(\strictRhoLo{}--\strictRhoHi{} on the pooled-score \strictRhoPooled{}) from $S = \strictS{}$ (\strictLo{}--\strictHi{};
$n = \strictN{}$) against its own $B' = \strictBprime{}$, read through $\rho$ alone because
$B$ at that $n$ is $\curveStrict{} \pm \curveStrictSd{}$; retention is not the artifact,
and the edit arm bounds $S$ under near-copies (appendix~\ref{app:robust}). A monitor is inflated the same way. The same
zero-shot trusted-monitor prompt scores \monStoredHead{} on the \emph{stored} pairs over the
same problems and \monitorS{} (\monitorSLo{}--\monitorSHi{}) on the headline pairs, a
monitor-$\rho$ of \monRhoHead{} (\monRhoHeadLo{}--\monRhoHeadHi{}) against the classifier's
\sameGenRho{}; on the strict pairs it is \monStoredStrict{} and \monitorStrict{}
(\monitorStrictLo{}--\monitorStrictHi{}), monitor-$\rho$ \monRhoStrict{}
(\monRhoStrictLo{}--\monRhoStrictHi{}), and a monitor that wrote neither class returns the
same two ratios (\monHaikuRhoHead{}, \monHaikuRhoStrict{}). Classifier and monitor are
independent pair by pair ($\phi = \monitorClfPhi{}$). Our single-shot attacks fire given a pass at \fireGivenPassOurs{} against the stored
attacker's \fireGivenPassStored{}: the stored attack class is a survivor set.

\paragraph{The filter installs a second artifact.}
Labelling by \texttt{backdoor\_works} defines the negative class by \emph{attack failure}
at the problem level rather than by honesty: \inertShare{} of the test split's labelled
backdoors do not work (\inertSharePassing{} of those that pass the public tests), and a
problem whose backdoor fails contributes its human honest solution to the negative class
instead, so the label is fixed before any code is read.
Attack problems carry roughly half as many test cases as honest ones
(\testCasesAttack{} versus \testCasesHonest{}), consistent with a problem with fewer tests
being easier to backdoor undetectably, so difficulty and test count are correlated with the
label with no code read at all --- which is where the content-blind floor of \nocodeCV{}
comes from.

\paragraph{The negative control comes free.}
In five same-generator agentic settings the same three models write both classes, so no
authorship confound is possible and any surviving floor is structural
(Figure~\ref{fig:corpora}, appendix): floors run from \bashFloor{} (\texttt{bash}, on its
null) to \agentdojoFloor{} (\texttt{agentdojo}) from character count alone. A per-benchmark floor separates
corpora where the label is trivially recoverable from those where it is not.

\section{Recommendations}
% Budget: ~190 words, in Kapoor & Narayanan's register.
% =====================================================================================

Following \citet{kapoor2022leakage}, who treat leakage as a checklist rather than a verdict:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Generate both classes with the same model and the same prompt framing.} If the attack class is
      model-written, the honest class must be too; as our arm-1 null shows, regenerating
      only one side substitutes one authorship gap for another.
\item \textbf{Report a partial-input floor and a bag-of-words floor beside every monitor
      number}, each against a permutation null.
\item \textbf{Do not define the negative class by attack failure.} Drop the problems
      whose attacks failed, or sample the negative class independently of attack success.
\item \textbf{State the split, the metric and the preprocessing} whenever monitors are
      compared, and \textbf{release the split and the scripts}. Ours accompany this paper under
      MIT: the split, both selection rules, the retry pool, the scripts, a card and a manifest
      (appendix~\ref{app:release}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
% Budget: three paragraphs. Disclosures owed by notes/05 are in the second.
% =====================================================================================

\textbf{A high floor is evidence about a corpus, not a verdict on results computed on it.}
\citet{feng2019misleading} warn that the \emph{failure} of a partial-input baseline does not
show a dataset is artifact-free; the converse binds us: our floors show the label is
recoverable without reading a backdoor \citep{gururangan2018annotation, poliak2018hypothesis},
not that any monitor exploits that.

\textbf{The same-generator arm is a later model behind a paid API.} The stored attacker,
\texttt{claude-3-7-sonnet-thinking}, is retired (404 against a live key), so both classes
come from a later model of the same lineage, called through a metered commercial API that
does not expose its sampling parameters: the arm is not bit-reproducible, and thinking was
off although the stored attacker was a thinking model. We do not length-match; the untrusted
model chooses its own trajectory length. The stored attacker and the same-generator attacker
also differ in backdoor style, not only in authorship, so every $\rho$ here --- classifier or
monitor --- bounds how much of the separability is content and does not partition it; and the
two generators are compared on the \texttt{passes} eligibility alone, Haiku 4.5's strict row
being refused at \haikuUsable{} usable pairs against our 50-pair floor.

\textbf{Scope, provenance and the harness.} Whether stored-pair corpora leak this way in
general is not established here: five of our seven corpora are one user's repackaging, and
claims are scoped to those releases. Two interpreter-era artifacts fall on opposite classes;
the same-generator arm is immune to them, $B$ is not (appendix~\ref{app:harness}).

\paragraph{Comment stripping on the matched-protocol numbers.}
\label{app:stripped}
The matched-pair and transfer measurements were first made with comments in. Every value in
the authorship paragraph of \S\ref{sec:results} was re-measured with the same stripper
applied to both classes, over five problem-disjoint splits (each transfer row is one fixed
split). With comments kept the same quantities were transfer \transferKept{}, working
\pairWorkingKept{}, inert \pairInertKept{}, all pairs \pairAllKept{} and surface-feature
AUROC \surfaceSevenKept{}; stripped they are \transfer{}, \pairWorking{}, \pairInert{},
\pairAll{} and \surfaceSeven{}. Every one was inflated by the comment channel, which is
this paper's thesis applied to itself. The \texttt{\#}-presence marker, \hashPresAttack{}
against \hashPresHonest{} before stripping, closes to \hashPresAttackStripped{} against
\hashPresHonestStripped{} after and is dropped; \texttt{\_\_main\_\_} is unchanged. One
authorship leak survives the stripper because string literals are not comments: in the
comment-stripped train split an author byline (\texttt{Author :}) survives in
\bylineHonest{} of \splitTrain{} honest samples against \bylineAttack{} attack samples, and
a triple-quoted block in \tripleHonest{} against \tripleAttack{} --- too rare to drive
\tfidfKoran{}, but a literal record of authorship.
Stripped, the matched-protocol TF-IDF AUROC is \tfidfMatched{}, beside $B = \baselineB$ and
the Koran-split \tfidfKoran{}, all three on stripped code. The other cross-generator

gemini-2.0-flash).

\paragraph{Robustness of the same-generator rows.}
\label{app:robust}
Intervals in the Gate S paragraph of \S\ref{sec:results} are bootstraps over problems,
resampling $S$ and $B'$ jointly for $\rho$; this replaced a $\pm2$ fold-sd rule before the
retry pool was read. Five fold seeds move the strict row's $\rho$ over \strictRhoSeeds{}.
Retention is not the artifact: survivors and non-survivors separate at
only \survivorAuroc{}, and $S$ with non-firing attempts on the same problems is
\survivorSwapS{} against \survivorSwapSref{}. The edit arm bounds $S$ under near-copies
(similarity \editSim{}, Table~\ref{tab:similarity}): \editS{} over \editN{} pairs,
$\rho = \editRho{}$. In arm 1, where only the honest half was regenerated, TF-IDF gains
\armOneTfidfDelta{} while the length carrier collapses to \armOneLenG{}. Half the gap
between our single-shot firing rate and the stored attacker's is our own attack prompt:
\trigPublicShare{} of our test-passing draws offer a public test input as the trigger, which
cannot diverge from the honest solution, and those fire \trigPublicFire{} against
\trigNovelFire{} for novel triggers; no stored working backdoor has such a trigger
(\trigStoredPublic{}). The rest is retry-and-keep.

\paragraph{The prompt-matched arm under the masks, and the second generator.}
\label{app:secondpass}
Under prompt matching the identifier mask --- the only mask of \S\ref{sec:results} that moved
$S$ --- takes the headline row to \matchedSid{} ($\rho = \matchedRhoId{}$) where it takes the
original honest class on the same \matchedN{} problems to \matchedSidOrig{}: most of what
identifier normalisation removed is what prompt matching removes. A character model
(3--5-grams, same classifier and folds) retains more than the word tokeniser, \matchedChar{}
($\rho = \matchedRhoChar{}$), so the drop is not an artefact of tokenisation. On the strict
pairs the matched row is $S = \matchedStrictS{}$ ($n = \matchedStrictN{}$,
$\rho = \matchedStrictRho{}$, \matchedStrictRhoLo{}--\matchedStrictRhoHi{}).
The second generator's pairs are more self-similar than the first's (\haikuPairSim{} mean
against \simIndep{}), so $S$ was recomputed inside similarity strata: it stays
\haikuStrataS{} in every stratum, including the \haikuStrataLowSim{}-similarity stratum,
which is further from its honest class than the stored benchmark's own pairs, while $B'$
holds \haikuStrataBprime{}. The collapse is not bought with near-copies. Its prompt-matched
rows move $S$ to \haikuSMatched{} ($\rho = \haikuMatchedRho{}$) and put the prompt-only floor
at \haikuPromptFloor{}, an interval containing chance.

\paragraph{The monitor rows, and what bounds the second monitor.}
The monitor that wrote neither class scores \monHaikuHead{} on the same-generator headline
pairs and \monHaikuStoredHead{} on the stored pairs over the same problems, \monHaikuStrict{}
and \monHaikuStoredStrict{} on the strict pairs. Its headline rows are a bound rather than a
point: a 512-token reply ceiling left \monHaikuMissHead{} same-generator replies unparseable,
and on the stored side the missing share differs by class (\monHaikuMissHonest{} honest
against \monHaikuMissAttack{} attack), a bias of unknown sign. Re-running the strict pairs
with a 1{,}024-token ceiling prices it: missingness falls to 2 of 198 and monitor-$\rho$ moves
from \monHaikuRhoStrict{} to \monHaikuRhoStrictMt{}, inside the bootstrap interval.

\paragraph{The two generators' funnel.}
Both generators were run through the identical pipeline. Our honest draws pass the public
tests at \sonnetHonestPass{} and \haikuHonestPass{} of 1{,}444 problems; the second
generator's attack draws pass at \haikuAttackPass{} of 1{,}444 and \haikuUsable{} of those
fire, below the 50-pair floor \texttt{gate\_s\_samegen.py} requires, which is why it has no
strict row and the two generators are compared on the \texttt{passes} eligibility only. The
single-shot firing rate is a property of the protocol rather than of either model:
\trigPublicShare{} of test-passing draws offer a public test input as their trigger.

\paragraph{The release.}
\label{app:release}
The supplementary bundle holds the same-generator split (\relHonest{} honest programs,
\relEdit{} edit-arm and \relIndep{} independent-arm attack programs written by one model,
plus every one of the \relRetryDraws{} draws of the independent arm's retry pool), both
eligibility rules as pair lists under an outcome-blind selection (\relPassFirst{} passing
and \relFireFirst{} firing independent-arm pairs) and under the benchmark's own survivor
selection (\relPassBest{} and \relFireBest{}, or \relFireBestCrash{} counting a crash on
the trigger as firing), the \relScripts{} scripts behind the measurements ---
generation for both generators, the test harness, the trigger verifier, the selection rules, the
prompt-matched arm, the monitor rows and each remaining measurement script --- a dataset card,
and a manifest of row counts and checksums. The licence, MIT, is a file inside the bundle and
covers the data and the scripts alike.

\paragraph{The test harness and the pass flag.}
\label{app:harness}
Gate S requires executing generated code, so we built a runner that feeds each shipped
input case to a solution under per-case timeouts and address-space limits. It agrees with
the artifact's own \texttt{solution\_passes\_tests} on \harnessAgreement{} of the analysis
pool and is strictly conservative there --- it never credits a solution the artifact rejects
(\harnessFailWePass{} such cases). We use \emph{our own} flag for both classes: a checker
stricter on one class than the other would manufacture exactly the artifact this paper
documents. The headline does not depend on that choice --- under the artifact's flag
$B = \baselineB$ at $n = \baselineBn{}$, under ours $B = \baselineBownflag{}$ at
$n = \baselineBownflagN{}$, within one standard deviation of each other. Our release
carries one MIT-licensed file of published monitor scores, with its notice; the monitor
scores in Figure~\ref{fig:monitors} are \texttt{runs/x0/monitor-summary.json} of that
release. Two interpreter-era artifacts in the upstream solutions fall on opposite classes: a
\texttt{stdin} without \texttt{.buffer}, fixed, and \texttt{fractions.gcd}, removed in
Python~3.9, not fixable.

\paragraph{The token test, run and failed.}
\label{app:token}
We attempted to confirm the token-level mechanism of \cite{orel2026aicd} in our setting and
it does not replicate: separation \tokenSep{} --- the wrong sign --- at permutation
$p = \tokenP{}$. The reason is register. Their human class is general-purpose repository
code, whereas \emph{both} of our classes are competitive-programming code, so an identifier
axis that separates general human code from model code does not separate these two
populations. Only 8 of their 10 canonical tokens are in our vocabulary, so the test is
underpowered; but the point estimate has the wrong sign, which is not what an underpowered
true effect looks like. We report it because a reader who knows that work may run exactly
this test.

\paragraph{A third channel, noted rather than developed.}
In the same five agentic settings a fifteen-word regular expression over the untrusted
policy's own scratchpad reaches \cotLow{}--\cotHigh{}, because prompted attack policies
restate their side task in the reasoning the monitor is given. We note this only to bound
the claim: the instrument belongs to \cite{little2026length}, which validates it properly
against an LLM judge and a length-matched null, and we defer to that treatment.
